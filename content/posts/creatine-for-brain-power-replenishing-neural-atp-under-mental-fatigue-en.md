## Biological mechanism: When neural cells starve for energy

The human brain accounts for only about two percent of body weight but consumes up to twenty percent of total body energy. To sustain thinking, remembering, and decision-making, neurons continuously synthesize the molecule Adenosine Triphosphate, known as ATP. When you focus intensely, ATP is consumed at a dizzying speed and converted into Adenosine Diphosphate, known as ADP, an exhausted form of energy. At this point, without sufficient Creatine stored as Phosphocreatine, brain cells fall into temporary energy starvation, causing mental fatigue, sluggishness, and lack of focus. Supplementing with Creatine increases the Phosphocreatine reserve pool right inside neurons, ready to donate a phosphate group to turn nutrient-poor ADP back into energy-rich ATP in a split second, maintaining stable cognitive performance even under heavy work pressure.

## Decoding scientific facts and practical performance

Many people still mistakenly believe Creatine only causes water retention in muscles or is exclusively meant for professional bodybuilders. However, modern neuroscientific studies have proven that brain tissue also possesses independent receptors and Creatine transport systems. When provided with adequate dosages, the brain significantly improves information processing capacity and minimizes mental fatigue during sleep deprivation or prolonged stress. ## Practical application and optimizing supplementation habits

To incorporate Creatine into your daily brain health regimen, you do not need massive doses like bodybuilders. The ideal dosage to support cognitive function and brain energy for working adults is three to five grams of pure Creatine Monohydrate daily, taken consistently in the morning or after intense work sessions. You can dissolve the powder in a glass of warm water, plain water, or fruit smoothie without altering its molecular structure. Furthermore, combining Creatine supplementation with a diet rich in nuts, fatty fish, and leafy greens will build a solid foundation to protect nerve cells from premature aging caused by oxidative stress.

Throughout the history of neurology, the boundary between mind and body was drawn sharply, with the brain acting as the supreme monarch autonomously deciding all human emotions, behaviors, and mental states. Every therapeutic approach for mood disorders like depression and anxiety revolved around direct pharmacological intervention across the blood-brain barrier to modulate serotonin or dopamine receptors. However, a major clinical paradox emerged when numerous patients failed to respond to traditional treatment regimens, while individuals with chronic gastrointestinal disorders frequently suffered from severe depression. The paradigm shift in medical thinking over the past decade has lifted the veil on the gut-brain axis, a critically important bidirectional communication system linking the digestive system and the central nervous system. The most groundbreaking highlight lies not in intestinal epithelial cells, but in the gut microbiota and the vagus nerve network. Recent empirical studies from leading journals like Nature and Cell have demonstrated that commensal bacteria such as Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium possess the capability to directly synthesize gamma-aminobutyric acid, the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter of the central nervous system. The long-standing common misconception that peripheral neurotransmitters cannot cross the intestinal barrier or affect the brain has now been debunked thanks to the mechanism of signal activation via the vagus nerve. This article will dissect this complex molecular pathway, deciphering how the gut microbiome orchestrates human mood through precise chemical and neurological pathways.

## 1. Micro-molecular mechanisms: Microbial GABA synthesis and afferent vagal signaling

The gut microbiome does not merely serve to assist in food digestion; it functions as an exceptionally sophisticated biopharmaceutical factory. Within this ecosystem, bacterial strains belonging to the genera Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium possess decarboxylase enzyme systems, notably glutamate decarboxylase (GAD). This enzyme catalyzes the metabolic conversion of L-glutamic acid, a common dietary excitatory amino acid, into gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the most vital inhibitory neurotransmitter of the central nervous system. Although peripheral GABA synthesized by bacteria cannot readily cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) due to restricted molecular permeability, it exerts potent physiological effects through the direct activation of receptors located on vagus nerve endings distributed throughout the intestinal mucosal layer. The vagus nerve acts as the primary information highway between the gut and the brain, with up to 80% of its nerve fibers being afferent fibers carrying visceral information upward to the brain. When peripheral GABA binds to specific GABA receptors or type 3 serotonin receptors (5-HT3) located on vagal nerve terminals, it triggers a cascade of electrochemical signals. These signals are conducted along vagal axonal fibers to the Nucleus Tractus Solitarii (NTS) located within the medulla oblongata. From the NTS, neural impulses propagate further to higher emotional and cognitive centers in the brain, including the amygdala, hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex. This process helps modulate the secretion of stress hormones such as cortisol while optimizing the activity of GABAergic neurons, creating a state of psychological homeostasis and mitigating symptoms of anxiety and depression.

## 2. Biological paradox and clinical đối chiếu of the gut-brain axis

One of the greatest paradoxes in modern clinical neurology is the phenomenon where centrally acting antidepressant medications fail to achieve optimal efficacy, whereas nutritional interventions or probiotic supplementation (psychobiotics) yield noticeable behavioral improvements. The core reason lies in the fact that diminished diversity within the gut microbiome (dysbiosis) leads to a severe depletion in the supply of endogenous microbial GABA. In the absence of vagal activation signals, the central nervous system falls into an imbalance between excitation and inhibition, with overactive glutamatergic pathways dominating, causing excitotoxicity in brain regions related to emotion. The table below provides a detailed comparison between the normal physiological state of the gut-brain axis and the pathological state under microbiome dysbiosis and disrupted vagal signaling.

| Physiological Parameter / Clinical State | Balanced State (Eubiosis & Intact Vagal Signaling) | Pathological State (Dysbiosis & Impaired Vagal Signaling) | Molecular Mechanism / Clinical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Density of GABA-producing Probiotics** | High (Predominantly Lactobacillus spp. and Bifidobacterium spp.) | Severely depleted (Proliferation of pro-inflammatory Proteobacteria) | Reduced GAD enzyme synthesis, deficiency of inhibitory neurotransmitter precursors. |
| **Vagal Signaling Activity** | Stable, regular afferent signaling transmission to the NTS | Substantially diminished, enhanced efferent pro-inflammatory signaling (weakened cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway) | Increased stress sensitivity, hyperactivation of the Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) axis. |
| **GABA Concentration in Cortex/Hippocampus** | Optimal, maintaining balance between excitation (Glutamate) and inhibition (GABA) | Asymmetric, elevated excitotoxicity | Induction of generalized anxiety disorders (GAD), treatment-resistant depression, and short-term memory impairment. |
| **Overall Clinical Manifestation** | Stable emotional state, high stress resilience, regulated digestion | Chronic anxiety, sleep disorders, irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) accompanied by cognitive decline | Bidirectional interaction between psychiatric symptoms and functional gastrointestinal disorders. |

## Practical Takeaways & Clinical Translation: 3. Clinical strategies and neuro-metabolic optimization

Building upon the molecular mechanistic foundations of the gut-brain axis and the role of the vagus nerve, modern management of neurological and mood disorders is entering an entirely new era. Rather than relying solely on selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) or benzodiazepine sedatives which carry numerous side effects, metabolic medicine practitioners are employing multimodal strategies to restore the gut microbial ecosystem. First, the application of specialized psychobiotic strains containing robust GABA-producing bacterial species has been clinically proven to significantly reduce anxiety scores in adults. Second, a dietary regimen rich in soluble fiber and prebiotics (such as inulin, FOS, GOS) supplies essential raw materials for microbial fermentation, promoting the production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) such as butyrate. These SCFAs not only nourish intestinal epithelial cells but also act directly on the Enteric Nervous System, upregulating GABA receptor expression and supporting the integrity of the intestinal barrier, thereby preventing bacterial toxins (lipopolysaccharides or LPS) from entering the systemic blood circulation and triggering chronic neuroinflammation. Finally, non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation interventions via deep breathing regulation techniques, meditative practices, or transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation devices targeting the auricular branch of the vagus nerve are emerging as powerful adjunctive tools. These methods help optimize vagal tone, re-establish smooth connectivity between the gastrointestinal tract and the brain, and provide a comprehensive solution for metabolic health, longevity, and sustainable mental well-being.

## Biological Mechanism: HEV Blue Light, Singlet Oxygen, and Retinal Membrane Phototoxicity

Digital screens emit High-Energy Visible (HEV) blue light within the 400 to 450 nm spectrum. This specific wavelength penetrates deeply through the cornea and crystalline lens, directly striking the macula where retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells are densely concentrated.

When RPE cells absorb excessive energy from blue light, photosensitizers such as A2E become excited, generating massive amounts of singlet oxygen (1O2) and reactive oxygen species (ROS). These volatile chemical entities aggressively attack polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) in the cell membrane.

This lipid peroxidation compromises membrane fluidity, impairing the accommodation capacity of ciliary muscles and leading to severe visual fatigue. Concurrently, circulating ROS upregulate MMP-1 matrix metalloproteinases in delicate periorbital skin, degrading dermal collagen and initiating fine lines.

## Scientific Decoding: Astaxanthin's Unique Transmembrane Architecture vs. Conventional Antioxidants

Not all antioxidants can effectively protect ocular and cutaneous tissues. The retina is shielded by the stringent Blood-Retinal Barrier (BRR), which restricts most hydrophilic molecules. Astaxanthin, a marine xanthophyll carotenoid, readily crosses this barrier due to its unique lipophilic affinity.

Astaxanthin's distinct advantage lies in its molecular geometry: two polar esterified heads linked by a non-polar conjugated polyene chain. This allows Astaxanthin to span vertically across the entire phospholipid bilayer, securing both the inner/outer membrane surfaces and the interior hydrophobic core.
